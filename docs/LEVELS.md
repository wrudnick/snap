# Locations

A location is not "more street". It is a set of photographs that were previously
impossible. Every entry here should be able to name the shots it unlocks.

## 1. Gold Coast — built

Michigan Avenue, the Magnificent Mile, and the streets behind it, at dawn through
dusk. Nine sections: Oak Street Beach, the underpass, East Lake Shore Drive,
Michigan Avenue, Delaware Place, Rush Street, the Triangle, the alley, and a
restaurant kitchen.

**What it is for.** Street life at close range — people, traffic, pigeons, dogs —
and the *bases* of famous buildings. Its defining limitation is that the streets
are narrow relative to the towers, so the great skyscrapers can be photographed
in detail but not entire. That limitation is deliberate and should not be
"fixed"; it is what makes the second location worth buying.

**Known weak points.** The alley runs within 0.9 m of a blank wall and needs
either clearance or something on the wall worth being close to. "Through the
Kitchen" is the least developed section.

## 2. The Playpen — confirmed, unbuilt

The boat anchorage off Oak Street Beach where everything rafts up on a summer
weekend. The rail is a boat's wake; you are a passenger.

**What it is for.** The skyline, entire. This is where the Hancock, the
Palmolive and the whole Gold Coast frontage finally fit in a frame, from far
enough back that Level costs you nothing. It is the postcard the game has been
withholding.

**Why it works here specifically.**

- It connects geographically to a beach section that already exists.
- The subject is a thing the game already owns and cannot currently sell.
- Boats, swimmers, gulls, jet-skis and party-goers are a subject roster that
  shares almost nothing with the street.
- **Swell.** The camera rises and falls, which makes a level horizon a genuine
  skill rather than a given — and earns image stabilisation a place in the shop.
- Water gives reflections and a low sun, which is most of what a postcard of a
  skyline is.

**Open.** Whether the boat's path is fixed like a rail or drifts; whether you can
be aboard a moving party boat or are on a tour launch.

## Candidates

Not scheduled. Recorded so they are not lost and not started.

- **The Riverwalk** — an architecture cruise is *literally* a postcard tour, and
  the river gives the same "far enough back" property as the lake with a canyon
  of buildings on both sides.
- **The L** — an elevated train is a rail in the most honest sense, and it puts
  you at third-floor height, which is a completely different set of framings.
- **Millennium Park** — the Bean is a mirror; reflections of the skyline in a
  curved surface are a scoring problem worth solving once.
- **Wrigleyville on a game day** — crowd density as the subject.
- **Pilsen** — murals. Flat, colourful, and the first location where the subject
  is a *surface* rather than a mass.
